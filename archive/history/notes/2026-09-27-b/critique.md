# Critique — Your Life as a Royal Physician in the Joseon Court

## Round 1

### Bedtime viewer
The cold open works and the thread-pulse crisis in Chapter Three is genuinely gripping without ever being loud. But there are real problems for a listener trying to drift off: the chapter breaks are abrupt — we jump from "seven years old" to "sixteen" to "inside the palace" to "years pass" with almost no breathing room, so it feels more like a summary than a lived night. Chapter Four's exile lands very fast and very cold right before the ending; a bedtime video needs more cushioning between "you are stripped of your rank" and the recovery that follows, or a listener drifts off mid-anxiety rather than mid-calm. The draft is also short of scenes (46 vs. the 55-70 called for) — several beats that deserve their own lingering, sensory scene (the walk into the palace for the first time, the actual moment the summons arrives, the return from exile) are compressed into a single line each.

### Historian
Checking every claim against research.md: the birth-status barrier, Hyeminseo apprenticeship, uigwa exam, ondol heating, robe-color ranks, the thread-and-screen pulse-taking, the uinyeo's role, moxa and ginseng, the water clock and curfew bell, the exile after the king's death, and the Hangul medical book at the end are all supported. Two things need tightening: "your green robe long since traded for blue" (Chapter Four) implies a mid-career promotion that research.md never actually documents for this character — keep it since ranks-by-robe-color is sourced (fact 23), but the specific promotion moment is invented business, which is fine as illustrative color, not a claim of fact. More importantly, "a new king comes to the throne, hears the truth... and sends word recalling you" compresses what research.md fact 16 describes as a contested restoration ("over many officials' objections") into something too clean and uncomplicated — the real record shows this pardon was resisted, and the script should at least gesture at that friction rather than presenting it as an easy happy turn.

### YouTube strategist
First thirty seconds: strong, specific stakes, no wasted breath — keep it. Retention risk: after the fever-crisis (Chapter Three), the emotional and narrative momentum actually flattens right into "Years pass, and you rise" — one of the two tense/urgent stretches the story needs is being spent on exile-grief rather than earned rising action, and there's no clear "small surprise fact" beat every ninety seconds in the back half; Chapter Four and Five both read a little too summary-driven, more told than shown. No AI-sounding stock words present ("tapestry", "delve", "testament" etc. — good, none used). Missing explicit chapter-end open loops — several chapter endings state what happens next rather than posing a felt, open question the viewer leans into. Title/thumbnail promise needs checking once thumbnails are drafted, but the content itself delivers on "your life as a royal physician" (this is not a generic "royal court" video), which is the differentiator trend research called for.

## Must fix
1. Expand from 46 scenes to the required 55-70 by giving standalone scenes to compressed beats: the palace summons arriving, your first walk through the palace gate, the exile journey itself, and the return from exile — each deserves its own lingering sensory scene rather than a single compressed line.
2. Soften the jump between Chapter One and Chapter Two — add a bridging scene of arrival at the palace gate (sound, smell, scale) before "Inside the palace, an elder physician warns you plainly," so the transition is felt, not just stated.
3. Add breathing-room scenes between "You are stripped of your rank" and "In exile you keep treating whoever finds their way to your door" — the fall needs at least one quiet, sad-toned scene of the journey away from the capital before recovery begins, so a listener isn't left on a jolt.
4. Fix the too-easy restoration: instead of "A new king comes to the throne, hears the truth of what happened, and sends word recalling you," show the new king's recall arriving against resistance — even one line acknowledging officials who still doubted you — so it matches research.md's "over many officials' objections" and earns the relief rather than handing it over.
5. Add at least one more clear surprise-fact beat in Chapter Two and Chapter Four (roughly every ninety seconds), framed as something you personally notice or are told, not a dry fact dropped in — for example the uinyeo's own precarious status (skilled physician, yet still a servant by law) deserves its own felt scene, not a passing mention.
6. Turn at least two chapter endings into genuine open loops (a felt, unresolved question) rather than statements of what happens next — for example the end of Chapter Two should leave the listener wondering whether you'll ever be tested, not just told you are "waiting."
7. Slow the ending further: the closing chapter currently moves from "you return" to "the book is finished" to "long after you are gone" within a handful of lines — stretch this into more scenes of the actual, physical work of writing (candlelight, aching hands, rereading old notes) before the time-jump to legacy, so the last minute stays slow and resolved rather than summarized.
8. Reconsider "your green robe long since traded for blue" — keep the rank detail (it is sourced) but attach it to a small remembered moment (the first time you are handed a blue sash) rather than a passing aside, so sourced facts read as lived experience, not narration of research.
9. Increase total word count toward the ~1,300-word target as scenes are added in points 1, 3 and 7 above.
