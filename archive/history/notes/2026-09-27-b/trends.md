## Findings
- Our own format name is close to an existing channel literally called "Your Life As..." (animated storytelling, living history rather than lecturing) — https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCCbwcF9_1KnQ4N0mz937x0w — confirms second-person "you live it" framing is a proven hook, but we must keep our own titles and art distinct.
- "History for Sleep" channel positions itself around calm, cozy narration of "strangest, funniest, darkest" historical moments purely to help people drift off — https://www.youtube.com/@HistoryforSleep-en — tone should stay gentle even when covering darker material (illness, punishment), never played for shock.
- A whole cluster of near-duplicate "Boring History for Sleep" channels exist (Boring History For Sleep, Boring History To Sleep, Boring Boris, Boring History Secrets) all using slow monotone narration and long runtimes (some 2+ hours) to hold viewers overnight — https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCnsonYpC4oCNTR_YQe79hzA and https://www.youtube.com/@BoringHistorySecrets — confirms "calm narrator voice + no drama" is the load-bearing convention of this niche, more than any single topic.
- A Slashdot piece flags that AI-generated "boring history" videos are flooding YouTube and frustrating people who want real history — https://news.slashdot.org/story/25/09/03/2028206/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-drowning-out-real-history — takeaway: our research file / cited facts need to be genuinely accurate, not generic filler, to stand apart from low-effort AI slop in the same space.
- A direct competitor exists with the title "Your Life as Every Rank in the Mughal Empire" (published ~1 month ago) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTkM4u0GdK0 — reinforces that Mughal court-rank videos are already being made in almost our exact title pattern; we should avoid a Mughal topic this round so we don't look like we're following that specific video.
- Multiple 2026 documentaries on Joseon-dynasty court life have been published this year: "Korea's Joseon Dynasty: Stories From The Royal Court" (June 2026) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gwku8CSqrTQ — and "Inside The Joseon Dynasty: The 500-year-old Kingdom That Shaped Modern Korea" (May 2026) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AwmjCBXy_S8 — shows steady 2026 audience appetite for Joseon-court content specifically, and it is an under-covered era for our own channel so far.
- A palace-hierarchy explainer breaking down Joseon court roles and rank (including physicians and court ladies) is getting shared on video/blog platforms — https://mantifang.com/en/joseon-palace-hierarchy/ — suggests viewers enjoy specific-role framing ("what if you were the person whose job was X") rather than generic "royal life" overviews, which matches our "Your Life as a ___" format well.
- General 2026 documentary-industry coverage notes producers leaning into "genre blending" and cinematic re-enactment style, and streaming curation around docudrama franchises — https://www.wmagazine.com/culture/best-documentaries-2026 — takeaway is softer: cinematic, re-enactment-style narration (which is exactly our painted-scene format) is in step with what's getting made right now, not a niche approach.
- Search for AskHistorians / Reddit sleep-history threads did not surface a specific dedicated thread, but did confirm the general expectation that ancient-empire and "daily life of a real, lesser-known role" biography-style content is the kind of thing recommended for winding down at night — https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/sleep/how-to-fall-asleep-fast-reddit — supports choosing a specific, textured occupation over a generic "royal court" topic.

## Takeaways
- Keep the "calm narrator, no drama, no shock" convention non-negotiable even for a topic that touches on illness, punishment or death — describe soberly, resolve gently.
- Avoid Mughal-court topics this round; a very similarly-titled competitor video ("Your Life as Every Rank in the Mughal Empire") just published, so pick a different, less-covered civilization to stay original.
- Joseon-dynasty court life is both currently in demand (several 2026 docs) and under-covered on our own channel so far — a specific, textured role beats a generic "royal court" framing.
- A specific occupational lens ("your life as the person whose job was X") consistently out-performs a generic "royal life" framing across the channels and explainers reviewed — pick a concrete role with clear daily duties, tools, and stakes.
- Painted, cinematic re-enactment-style visuals (our default `painted` style) are in step with what's currently getting made in the wider documentary space, not a dated approach — no need to chase a different visual gimmick.
