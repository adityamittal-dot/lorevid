# Critique — Your Life as a Temple Dancer in the Chola Empire

## Bedtime viewer
- Chapter Four stacks three fear-beats back to back: "Your hands go cold at the news," "you cannot remember a single step," and "for one terrifying breath you cannot make your feet begin." That's a spike, not a rise — it jolts rather than tenses gently, and repeats the same idea three times.
- "the whole street of dancers goes suddenly, unnaturally quiet" at the end of Chapter Three reads faintly like a horror beat ("unnaturally") rather than a hushed, expectant pause — wrong register for a video meant to ease someone toward sleep.
- "the whole courtyard erupts" after the performance is too sudden and loud an image right before bedtime; the video needs to come down gently even at its most triumphant moment, not spike volume.
- I do feel like I'm there in the sensory chapters (jasmine, ghee, cold tank water, gold anklets), but Chapter Five's Chellam-marries-a-merchant beat is a brand-new thread introduced with no room to land — it pulls focus away from the girl I've been following right when I want the story to settle, not widen.
- Overall runtime (about eleven minutes fifty at narration pace) runs long for the stated ten-minute bedtime format; by the midpoint I noticed the pacing sagging slightly rather than gliding.

- "the songs called Thevaram" (Chapter Two) — true and well documented (Thevaram is the canon of seventh-to-eighth century Tamil Shaiva hymns), but research.md does not currently carry a numbered fact for it. Must add a sourced Thevaram fact to research.md before the claim stays in the script.
