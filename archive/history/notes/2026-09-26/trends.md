## Findings
- Our own format ("Your Life As...", second-person, discover what your life would have been) already has a dedicated channel, confirming demand for the exact hook. — https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCCbwcF9_1KnQ4N0mz937x0w
- The "history for sleep" niche is crowded with near-identical channel names (History For Sleep, Strange History for Sleep, Boring History For Sleep, Dozing Story, Relaxing Boring History For Sleep, Boring History To Sleep, History & Sleep, Sleepy History) — differentiation has to come from the second-person angle, not the name. — https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCnsonYpC4oCNTR_YQe79hzA
- A direct competitor already runs a "Your Life as Miyamoto Musashi" style episode on a boring-history-for-sleep channel, framed as an "uneventful, overly detailed" life — confirms the format is being copied, so our titles/angles must stay original and not mirror theirs. — https://historyisboring.com/castle-life/medieval-superstitions/
- "Boring History For Sleep" titles favour a hook + colon/pipe pattern, e.g. "Boring History For Sleep | Why You Wouldn't Last a Day in Medieval Times" and "...Forgotten Medieval SURVIVAL Techniques We Ignore" — direct-address, myth-busting framing performs well. — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9jnekLeHz3c
- The genre is explicitly built on being "intentionally underwhelming" — slow, dry, steady narration with mundane detail, "just enough to keep your brain from wandering but not enough to keep you awake" — validates our calm, unhurried draft style over anything punchy. — https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC8F-FVe64Aj-tUkJsIGtSSw
- Long-form 1.5-2 hour "boring history" compilations are common, but our ~10 minute single-story format is closer to a bedtime short story than a compilation — worth leaning into a complete emotional arc rather than a list of facts. — https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL1EJgJ6XIO3zzERZIOURWnWBPR52h1vYv
- Rome/Vikings/medieval "what life was like" content is dominated by short animated-documentary explainers (Kings and Generals, HISTORY channel), not narrative second-person storytelling — our niche is still open on the narrative side. — https://www.history.com/videos/life-of-a-viking
- Gladiator content is very saturated (multiple recent "day in the life of a gladiator" uploads in the last year across big channels) — freshness is lower for that topic right now. — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EE92jpqicz0
- Thumbnail research confirms the emotional-close-up-face + bold 3-5 word sans-serif text formula performs best: a 500-video study found 69% of breakout videos used a face (80% of the biggest overperformers), and 89% used a face or high contrast — matches our fixed thumbnail layout. — https://vidiq.com/blog/post/types-youtube-thumbnails/
- Reddit and sleep-podcast coverage (Sleep With Me, Nothing Much Happens) shows the winning sleep-content formula is a flat, unhurried, slightly rambling voice over a gentle narrative rather than dense facts — reinforces "write for the ear," fewer facts per minute, more sensory dwelling. — https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/11-podcasts-help-fall-asleep-165200428.html

## Takeaways
1. Don't reuse a "Boring History For Sleep"-style title or their exact hook phrasing; our differentiator is a genuine second-person narrative arc, not a fact list.
2. Gladiators, Vikings and generic "medieval peasant" life are heavily covered elsewhere this week — pick an angle with a specific, less-mined historical event as the spine (not just "a day in the life").
3. Keep pacing slow and sensory rather than fact-dense; one clear emotional arc beats many facts, matching what sleep audiences actually say they want.
4. Thumbnail should stay disciplined: one strong emotion on a face, 2-4 bold words, one yellow highlight — do not over-decorate.
5. Recent days already used Japan (samurai's wife) and Mughal India (elephant keeper), so today should move to a different region/period for freshness.
