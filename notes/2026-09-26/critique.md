# Critique — Your Life as a Medieval Peasant Woman After the Black Death

## Bedtime viewer
- The hook works but "By the time the bell rings for Nones" leans on an unexplained canonical-hours term; a half-asleep viewer has to work out what "Nones" means in the first ten seconds, which is the worst possible place to introduce friction.
- "Empty" is used constantly — "half the cottages... stand with their doors open to the wind" (2), "the empty houses" (4), "empty land" (6, 12), "empty fields" (26), "the empty half of the village" (chapter title) — by chapter 3 the repetition itself starts to feel like clutter rather than atmosphere.
- The childbirth sequence is the right emotional peak, but "you stop being afraid of dying and start being afraid only of the silence that would follow if the child did not cry" is genuinely tense — right for a midpoint, but it needs a softer landing sentence immediately after the birth so a listener drifting toward sleep isn't left on the scary image a beat too long.
- Several sentences explain a historical mechanism rather than living inside a moment — "the reeve reads a new order aloud at the church door: no labourer may take wages higher than before the plague" (25) reads like a caption, not something she feels in her hands or her stomach.
- Chapter 1 has no real "turn" or surprise in its middle — scenes 5 through 9 all restate "there is more land now" in slightly different words instead of giving one vivid, specific image the ear can hold onto.

## Historian
- Scene 3 places the protagonist's marriage negotiations (chapter 2) starting "by midsummer" of the same year she is introduced at sixteen, but `research.md` fact 20 states peasants "typically married later than nobles, often in their twenties." As written, the timeline contradicts our own source — this must be fixed by aging up the marriage or adding a clear time-skip.
- Scene 44, "The statute that froze your wages is never formally undone, and yet somehow... it stops being enforced with any teeth," states as flat fact what `research.md` fact 5 explicitly marks "(debated)". This needs a hedge ("as far as anyone in the village could tell," or similar), not a confident historian's verdict delivered by a peasant narrator.
- Scene 42's account of Tyler's death "before the whole crowd's eyes" is consistent with `research.md` fact 9 and the wider historical record (Smithfield, in view of the king and crowd) — accurate, no fix needed, but flagging since it's the one moment a viewer might fact-check.
- No claim in the draft is unsupported by `research.md` — every named event (Statute of Labourers, merchet, the Peasants' Revolt, Cuxham manor rolls, pottage, the wage freeze) traces to a numbered fact. The one structural fix needed is the marriage-age timeline above.

## YouTube strategist
- First 30 seconds: strong stakes, but "Nones" should be replaced with plain language — jargon in the cold open risks losing exactly the sleepy, half-attentive viewer this channel exists for.
- No AI-cliché words found (no "tapestry," "testament," "delve," "little did you know," "in a world where") — clean on that front.
- Chapter 3's ending ("you have already chosen her name, and quietly decided she will never once have to pay merchet") is emotionally satisfying but is a closed statement, not an open loop — it resolves the merchet theme early instead of pulling the viewer into chapter 4's unrest. It should instead gesture toward what is coming (the poll tax, the roads filling with angry men) so the chapter break earns its cliffhanger.
- Read aloud, scenes 53 and 57 run long and clause-heavy for a slow bedtime pace — they will need either a comma-break rebalance or a trim so the narrator has a natural place to breathe.
- Word count on this draft (~1,526 words in the scene lines) runs to roughly 11.7 minutes at bedtime pace — over the 9.5–10.5 minute target — so the rewrite needs a real trim, not just line edits, particularly in chapter 1's repetitive middle stretch.
