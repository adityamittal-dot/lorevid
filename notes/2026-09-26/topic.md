**Chosen:** Your Life as a Medieval Peasant Woman in an English Village After the Black Death
**Angle:** Not a generic "day in the life," but a real historical hinge: you survive the plague year that
emptied half your village, inherit more land and a rarer kind of freedom than any peasant before you, marry
under the old manorial rules (merchet, dowry), face the lord's attempt to freeze your family's new wages
under the Statute of Labourers, live through the unrest of 1381, and grow old in a village that is smaller,
harder-worked, and — in one quiet way — more your own than your mother's ever was. This keeps the "your life
as" promise (full arc, not a fact-dump), gives three real anchor events (Black Death aftermath, Statute of
Labourers, Peasants' Revolt) the historian critic can check against `research.md`, and stays firmly in
bedtime-comfort territory: hardship described soberly, resolution warm.
