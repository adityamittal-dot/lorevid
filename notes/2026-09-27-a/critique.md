# Critique — Your Life as a Maratha Cavalry Soldier

## Round 1

### Bedtime viewer
The opening line — "Before sunrise you are only a farmer's son, standing barefoot on cracked earth, listening to a horse breathe in the dark." — is calm and sensory, but it has no stake or question, and the brief is explicit that the first three sentences need a vivid moment *plus* a stake. As written I don't yet know what I stand to lose or gain, so the hook is soft. A few expository lines ("it is said the Peshwa knows the enemy's numbers before the enemy has finished counting them") read like a documentary aside rather than something I am living through — they pull me slightly out of the first person. The heirloom motifs (the red thread, the brass token) are lovely but both appear so often, in near-identical phrasing, that by chapter 5 they start to feel like a checklist being ticked rather than something felt. The midpoint gut-punch (the dead boy by the river) lands well and is followed by real quiet, which is good bedtime pacing. The ending is appropriately slow and warm — no shocks in the last minute.

### Historian
Checking every claim against `research.md`:
- "Bahirji Naik's men move ahead of the column disguised as pilgrims and grain merchants" (Chapter 2, on the march to Palkhed) — this is a real anachronism. Bahirji Naik was Shivaji's spymaster and was active in the 1650s–1670s; Shivaji died in 1680. The Battle of Palkhed was fought in 1728 under Peshwa Baji Rao I, roughly half a century after Bahirji Naik's era. Naming him here places a specific, real historical figure fifty years after his documented lifetime — the single biggest factual problem in the draft.
- "disguised as pilgrims and grain merchants" is also more specific than `research.md` supports — fact 14 only says spies were "disguised as ordinary travelers," not specifically pilgrims or grain merchants.
- Everything else checks out against the file: the bargir/shiledar distinction (fact 1), the Bhimthadi horse's Arabian-and-local breeding (fact 8), the discipline code protecting women, children, Brahmins and farmland and requiring booty to go to the treasury (facts 16–17), the campaign rations of gram, rice, gur and flour (fact 20), the dry-season/monsoon campaign rhythm (fact 19), the Palkhed encirclement cutting off the Godavari and forcing a bloodless surrender (facts 9–10), and Bhavani as the clan deity soldiers invoked (fact 29). Good — no invented pay figures were used, respecting the note that fact 23's wages are debated.

Title/thumbnail work hasn't been drafted yet — flagging to make sure the thumbnail promise (a life-or-death cavalry moment) matches what the script actually delivers (it does — Palkhed's river standoff is the money scene).

2. Also soften "disguised as pilgrims and grain merchants" to match `research.md`'s more general "disguised as ordinary travelers," since the specific cover identities aren't sourced.
3. Rewrite the opening three sentences of Chapter 1 so the very first lines carry a clear stake or question, not just a calm image — per the brief's own example ("By sunset, you will know if he is coming home").
4. Trim the over-length scenes flagged by the strategist (several run 29–35 words against an 18–28 target), especially the run of long scenes in Chapters 4 and 5 — split some into two shorter beats rather than cutting content.
5. Vary the repeated "thread of red cotton around your wrist" phrasing on its second and third uses so the motif reads as an echo rather than a copy-pasted line.
6. Add two or three more scenes (currently 54, target 55–70), ideally breathing-room beats around the Palkhed battle and in the final chapter, to ease the pacing problem from note 4 without deleting story.
7. Tighten "it is said the Peshwa knows the enemy's numbers before the enemy has finished counting them" and similar documentary-style asides so they read as something the viewer notices/feels in the moment, not narration about the war in general.
8. Chapter 2's closing line ("the whole column turns without a single word of complaint") is a weak open loop — sharpen it so it makes a concrete promise about what's coming (Palkhed) rather than just describing obedience.
9. Double-check no chapter has two "tense"/"urgent" scenes bracketing an unrelated calm scene in a way that breaks the calm-mostly rule — Chapter 3 is correctly the one high-tension chapter; keep the surrounding chapters mostly calm/warm/reflective as already drafted.
