# Critique — Your Life as a Royal Falconer in a Medieval Court

## Round 1

### Bedtime viewer
- Chapter 4 stacks several high-adrenaline beats back to back: the storm breaking, the frantic call-and-lure, the mention of law-book punishment, and the all-night search. A listener drifting toward sleep gets jolted rather than gently carried through the low point.
- The line "a stolen or lost falcon can cost a man his eyes in the old law books" is a genuinely unsettling image to land on right before the emotional low of the story — too sharp for a bedtime tone.
- The very first line, "today the old falconer chooses his last apprentice," is a fact, not a feeling — it tells me what is happening rather than putting me in the muddy square smelling woodsmoke and straw. I don't feel "there" until several lines in.
- The jealous courtier is introduced with real menace in Chapter 3 ("begins asking loudly what a servant knows that lords do not") and then simply never mentioned again — I keep waiting for that thread to resolve and it doesn't, which is a nagging, unrelaxing loose end for a sleep video.
- "you never once raise your voice inside these walls" appears almost word for word in Chapter 1 and Chapter 5 — right now it reads like an accidental repeat rather than an intentional echo of how far you've come.

### Historian
- "worth by royal decree more than a knight earns in a year" is applied to the king's gyrfalcon, but research.md's decree (fact 3) values a trained *peregrine* at one thousand marks — the draft borrows the number for the wrong species without saying so.
- "a stolen falcon's nest carries its own harsh price" and the later "cost a man his eyes" line both come from research.md facts about *poaching a wild falcon's nest* (fact 8, fact 9) — the draft applies wild-nest poaching law to a royal falconer's own accident of losing a trained hawk in a storm, which research.md never claims. These are two different legal situations and the draft blurs them.
- "a seat near the high table" is vague where research.md gives an exact, striking detail: a royal falconer historically sat fourth at the king's table after the sovereign (fact 18). The draft leaves this specific unused.
- "the hunt at Ashden Wood" is an invented place name presented with the same factual weight as everything sourced from research.md — nothing in research.md supports a named location, so this should not be stated so specifically.
- The single richest, most citable fact in research.md — the Boke of Seynt Albans hierarchy assigning a hawk species to every rank (fact 1) — is completely unused in the draft. This is the fact most worth dramatizing.

### YouTube strategist
- First three sentences don't yet deliver "a vivid moment plus a stake" per the format's own retention rule — right now it's two sentences of setup before any personal stake lands.
- No AI-sounding stock phrases found ("tapestry," "testament," "delve," etc.) — good, keep it that way in the rewrite.
- Chapter 3 ends on a strong open loop into the storm ("the one thing you have learned to love most") — keep this, it works.
- The single biggest emotional moment (finding the lost hawk) currently lands around scene 49 of 65 — closer to the three-quarter mark than the midpoint the retention rule calls for. The first flight before the king (~scene 29) is a natural candidate for the true midpoint high and should be treated as such.
- At roughly 1,558 words, the draft runs closer to twelve minutes than the ten the format is built for; it needs trimming to land back in the 1,250–1,350 word bedtime range.

## Must fix
1. Quoting "today the old falconer chooses his last apprentice" — open on a sensory image (woodsmoke, wet straw) fused with the personal stake in the first two sentences, not a flat statement of fact.
2. Quoting "worth by royal decree more than a knight earns in a year" — either attach this valuation to a peregrine specifically or drop the precise number for the king's gyrfalcon so the claim matches research.md's actual source fact.
3. Quoting "a stolen falcon's nest carries its own harsh price" and "cost a man his eyes in the old law books" — separate the wild-nest poaching penalty (research.md fact 8–9) from an honest court falconer's accident; do not imply the protagonist himself risks blinding for losing a hawk in a storm.
4. Quoting "a seat near the high table opens for a falconer" — replace with the sourced, more specific detail that a royal falconer sat four seats from the king himself (research.md fact 18).
5. Quoting "the hunt at Ashden Wood" — remove the invented place name; use an unnamed, generic royal forest instead.
6. Work the Boke of Seynt Albans hawk-by-rank hierarchy (research.md fact 1: gyrfalcons for kings, goshawks for yeomen, kestrels for servants) into Chapter 1, since it is the richest unused research fact.
7. Quoting "begins asking loudly what a servant knows that lords do not" — give the jealous courtier a clear resolution later in the script instead of dropping the subplot after Chapter 3.
8. Chapter 4 currently stacks storm, chase, blame-threat and all-night search with no calmer beat between them — soften at least two of these beats (vary tone to sad/tender/reflective between the tense ones) so a bedtime listener isn't jolted awake.
9. Trim the whole script by roughly 200–250 words so total length lands near 1,300 words instead of 1,558.
10. Quoting "you never once raise your voice inside these walls" (Chapter 1) repeated near-verbatim in Chapter 5 — keep as a deliberate callback but vary the wording slightly so it reads as intentional, not accidental.
11. Shift the treatment of the biggest emotional beat so the first flight before the king (near the true midpoint) carries the peak emotional charge the retention rule calls for, with the storm/reunion in Chapter 4 as a strong second beat rather than competing for "biggest."
