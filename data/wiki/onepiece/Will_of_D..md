# Will of D.

Source: https://onepiece.fandom.com/wiki/Will_of_D. (fetched 2026-10-20). Bracketed [Ch. N] / [Ep. N] are the wiki's own citations.

The "Will of D." is a form of inherited will surrounding members of the Clan of D. [Ch. 763, Ep. 702] who bear the initial "D." in their names.
It is said that the Clan is the Natural Enemy of God [Ch. 764, Ep. 703] and is opposed to the World Government, namely its World Nobles, though most members do not know its true meaning. [Ch. 1085, Ep. 1119] Gol D. Roger, who learned its meaning during his voyage to Laugh Tale, is considered to be a central figure concerning the Will of D, with much discussion about those who have since borne the initial centering on them inheriting Roger's will. More often than not, people in the Clan of D. have been noted to be regularly involved in world-shaking events.

### The Clan of D.
According to Nerona Imu, the D. was once a label used by enemies of the First Twenty, who were thus presumably aligned with the Great Kingdom. Donquixote Rosinante explained to Trafalgar D. Water Law that the "D." has been secretly inherited worldwide throughout history and that in a certain land, the Clan of D. was referred to as the "Natural Enemy of God", with "God" referring to the Celestial Dragons, who are considered to be gods. In Mary Geoise, World Noble children were told that if they misbehaved, they would be eaten up by "D". Similarly, elders of that country seemed quite wary of the "storm" that those with "D." were known to cause.
However, Imu has stated that present-day carriers of the initial usually do not know about its origins and meaning. Jaguar D. Saul displayed a lack of knowledge on the subject when Nico Robin asked him what it meant. [Ch. 392, Ep. 275] Similarly, when Robin brought up the significance of the initial to Luffy, he was completely clueless as to what she was talking about. [Ch. 180, Ep. 111] Luffy and Ace themselves never thought much about their names during their childhood, in spite of Sabo pointing out the obvious coincidence. Gol D. Roger is the first and, as of yet, only known "D."-bearer in the modern era storyline to have discovered its meaning. [Ch. 576, Ep. 485]
The moniker of D. is something that is passed down through families whose ancestors bore it. [Ch. 392, Ep. 275] [Ch. 432, Ep. 314] It does not appear as though the various families in the Clan of D. are all blood-related, given that they come from multiple races (with there being at least one D-bearing family from the giants race) and that members from different families have had children together like Roger and Rouge.
Out of the several families that have passed down the D, the Davy Family has stood out in terms of their continued active enmity with the World Government. Imu has acknowledged that he has a grievance with the family's ancestor, Davy D. Jones, and the government was shown to take immediate action to massacre those of Davy blood found to be living in hiding on God Valley. One descendant, Rocks D. Xebec, demonstrated knowledge of the idea that Jones' will would be passed down through his descendants and would ultimately culminate in the fulfillment of a promise that was made, which Xebec implied would have a negative outcome for Imu. [Ch. 1164]
Imu has also equated the land of giants, Elbaph, with the D. Although there are currently no known natives of the land who are confirmed to be part of the clan, Elbaph has possessed a deep enmity with the World Government that has run for centuries. When King Harald submitted himself to the World Government out of genuine remorse for the giants' defiance and wishing to reconcile, it ended with Harald defying Imu, causing Imu to suffer a backlash that reminded him history does not lie about the fact that the Clan of D. and the World Government will never be compatible. [Ch. 1171]
During his posthumous broadcast to the world about the Void Century, Dr. Vegapunk spoke concerning the Clan of D. and told them that among them there is "a"—or possibly "another"—but his message was temporarily cut off right then, and his words were not heard. [Ch. 1117, Ep. 1150]

#### Traits of the Clan
Members of the Clan of D. have been shown possessing many similar traits, in particular a degree of fearlessness and an inclination toward action, even when said actions may impact the entire world. Just about all known members of the Clan of D. have held leadership positions and roles. True to their reputation as world shakers, clan members have frequently been central figures in the world's greatest conflicts and incidents, and more often than not, multiple members have been involved; interestingly, members have at times been on different sides and directly opposed each other. These tremendous events have included the Great War, the God Valley Incident, the Ohara Incident, the Duel on Banaro Island, the Celestial Dragon Hit-and-Run Incident, the Impel Down Great Escape Incident, the Summit War of Marineford, the Raid on Onigashima, and the Egghead Incident. Members of this clan can be found scattered all over the Blue Planet, with their presence known in the Four Blues and the Grand Line.
Although most of them do not know the historical significance of their name, Clan of D. members have frequently demonstrated animosity toward their supposed natural enemy, the World Nobles, and an inclination to oppose them despite their great status. The first known clan member, Lili, sabotaged the attempts of the nobles' ancestors, the First Twenty, to cover up the events of the Void Century by spreading the Poneglyphs across the world. Luffy directly caused the aforementioned Celestial Dragon Hit-and-Run Incident by punching Saint Rosward Charlos with no regard for the consequences, and his father, Dragon, actively seeks to overthrow the Celestial Dragons from power. [Ch. 904, Ep. 880] Though he is a high-ranking member of the Marines, Garp is noted to be strongly opposed to the World Nobles and rejected numerous offers to be promoted beyond Vice Admiral rank because doing so would put him directly under their authority. [Ch. 957, Ep. 958] For much of his life, Law possessed a vendetta to kill Donquixote Doflamingo, albeit Doflamingo had long lost his noble status; Rocks D. Xebec put many Celestial Dragons under threat during the God Valley Incident. During the God Valley Incident, Xebec, Roger, and Garp came together to attack Imu after he possessed Jaygarcia Saturn with eager excitement and no hesitation. [Ch. 1163]
In situations where defeat or even death appears inevitable, many D. carriers have had a knack for accepting their fates without fear and even expressing some degree of happiness or contentment. This trait was explicitly noted by Smoker when he witnessed Luffy laughing in Loguetown as Buggy was about to execute him, which Roger had done at that spot in the same circumstances 22 years earlier. [Ch. 99, Ep. 52] Another notable instance occurred when Roger's son Ace died on Marineford, as he died with a smile on his face after thanking Luffy and his crew for loving him as family. [Ch. 574, Ep. 483] Such behavior is not exclusive to D. carriers, of course, and other clan members have demonstrated greater fear in the face of danger, like Clover [Ch. 1120, Ep. 1152] [Ch. 396, Ep. 277] and particularly Blackbeard. [Ch. 576, Ep. 485]
As many things as there are that link them together, it is apparent that each clan member still possesses unique dispositions and individual agencies and are not all bound toward the same destiny. Edward Newgate said as much when he told Blackbeard that he was not the one Roger was waiting for to inherit his will, although he did seem to believe that the one who does fulfill Roger's will and find the One Piece will be connected to the D. [Ch. 576, Ep. 485]

### Carriers of the Initial "D."

#### Carriers' Profile

### The Will of D. in History
The initial D. is indicated to have been adopted around the beginning of the Great War during the Void Century 800 to 900 years ago as a moniker of opposition to the alliance that founded the World Government. The earliest known carriers of the initial, Nefertari D. Lili and Davy D. Jones, lived around this time. In spite of being part of the First Twenty that founded the World Government, Lili ultimately conspired against her own side for unknown reasons, leading to the scattering of Poneglyphs around the globe. Since then, the Nefertari Family, has continuously followed Lili's will to protect the Poneglyphs through a letter of her authoring, which has been passed down among generations of Arabasta rulers. Davy D. Jones is believed by his descendants to have been the King of the World at one point, which is disputed by the World Government's sovereign, Imu. Jones and Imu had a great enmity, and this has continued for the subsequent eight centuries as the World Government has sought to hunt down anyone of the Davy bloodline. According to Jones' descendant Rocks D. Xebec, a promise was made that Davy's will would be passed down and eventually come for Imu. Emet appears to be familiar with the Will of D, going as far as enacting Joy Boy's plan to protect the life of someone who carries it. [Ch. 1122, Ep. 1153]
After visiting Laugh Tale, Kouzuki Oden mentioned that he and the Roger Pirates had learned the meaning of the D. [Ch. 967, Ep. 968] After that, Roger noted the World Government was hiding his true name, Gol D. Roger, but considering everything he and his crew learned in Laugh Tale, he understands the reason for this conspiracy. During their final meeting, Roger was asked by Whitebeard what "D." is, and Roger appeared to have told him. Whitebeard later noted during the Summit War of Marineford that killing someone with a "D." would not extinguish the flames of their will because someone would eventually inherit that will and carry on Roger's wish.
Kureha was the first person in the series to mention the "Will of D." when she revealed that the true name of the late Pirate King was Gol D. Roger. She told Dalton that Chopper had joined up with a very dangerous man. [Ch. 154, Ep. 91]
The "Will of D." has also been mentioned by Nico Robin three times, the first being when she rescued Luffy from quicksand during Operation Utopia; she asked Luffy about the meaning of the "D." and why those with the name fight, but he had no idea. The second was when Robin was talking to Gan Fall after they pulled up the ancient belfry. Gan Fall mentioned feeling that Luffy left the same impression on him as Gol D. Roger did. [Ch. 301, Ep. 194] The third time was when Robin tried to ask Silvers Rayleigh about the significance of the "Will of D.", bringing up the Void Century at the same time. He did not reply to what the "D." stood for but stated that they (the Roger Pirates) had discovered the details of the Void Century; however, Robin declined his offer to tell her about it, determined to discover the truth herself. [Ch. 507, Ep. 400]
Later on, Trafalgar Law wondered aloud that the Will of D. will almost certainly call up a storm again. It is still not clear exactly what he meant, but it hints that Silvers Rayleigh had a clear purpose for wanting to find Monkey D. Luffy on Amazon Lily. [Ch. 591, Ep. 507] After the timeskip, Law mentioned the Will of D. once more when Doflamingo asked him why he had so much faith in Luffy. [Ch. 729, Ep. 661]

### Knowledge of D. in the World
In contrast to the openness of Luffy and other "D"-bearers, there are some "D." families who seem to know its significance, if not its meaning, and try to keep it secret from the public, such as Law's. [Ch. 763, Ep. 702] Overall, despite the historical animosity between them and the Clan of D., the World Government has typically taken no action against these families (with the notable exception of the Davy Family), and some of their members have gone on to serve the Marines in very prominent positions. In most cases, the government does not seem to have any issue with the moniker being printed on wanted posters either. Persecution of those bearing the D. does exist though, as Clou D. Clover lost his brother to unknown assailants and had to lie about not possessing the initial in order to survive. It seems that some high-ranking Marines have some awareness of the initial's significance, as Sengoku showed interest in Ace because of it. [Ch. 552, Ep. 461] When Sengoku was explicitly asked by Law if he knows about the meaning of the "D.", he gave a vague answer, but seemed shocked to find out that Law also carries the initial and thought about the checkered fate of the "D." carriers. [Ch. 798, Ep. 743]
However, actions will be taken to cover up the moniker in particularly extreme cases concerning people who are extremely famous worldwide or delve into actions or subjects considered taboo. The most notable case was with Roger, who was renamed "Gold Roger" by the press upon becoming Pirate King; nowadays, most people in the world do not know that those with the D. bear the same initial as the Pirate King. When Luffy awakened the Gomu Gomu no Mi and took the form of Nika, the Five Elders ordered that the D in his name be removed from his updated wanted poster, unsuccessfully, after seemingly having no issue with the initial being printed in his six previous posters. Rocks D. Xebec also had his full name hidden and is known by most as simply "Rocks". After Portgas D. Ace became the central focus of the Summit War of Marineford, Ethanbaron V. Nusjuro said the prominence of the D. in his name posed a greater danger than any other global impact of the war and feared it becoming too well known among the world. [Ch. 594, Ep. 511]
